PSA Swaps Cards

How One Customer Lost a ~$1,000 Card Entrusting PSA With His First-Ever Card Grading Submission

This website is owned and operated by a US citizen who submitted a ~$1,000 English Cloud Kingdom Hearts card in pristine condition to PSA. PSA swapped my card with a less than $100 Japanese version of the card in significantly inferior condition. This site exists to warn other customers about PSA's practices and to encourage precautions before submitting your cards for grading.

When I first noticed this swap on PSA's site, I immediately notified PSA with an error report. PSA ignored my error report, and I reached out after a month with no contact while my card moved to grading according to their site. I initiated an investigation on that call when they said, “Yeah, your card's Japanese.” The investigation concluded that I sent a Japanese card. I did not; I could not have submitted a card I have never owned. But PSA concluded from their investigation — incorrectly and impossibly — that I sent a Japanese card. I have never owned a Japanese Kingdom Hearts card. I only ever owned the English Cloud card, which my brother gave me about 20 years ago as a gift, back when we were more into Kingdom Hearts.

When a formal dispute was filed with PSA's parent company, PSA provided non-evidence: blurry photos with no timestamps that literally proved nothing except that they have a Japanese card (presumably the one they swapped mine with) and my box via USPS. I'd post the photos, but for now I'm respecting their legal ownership of them, unlike their total disrespect of my legal ownership of the card I sent to them.

PSA refused to provide the evidence that would actually prove their claim: video recording of the card I submitted being removed from my untampered box. They first ignored my request for it, and then rejected it:

“We are unable to share unedited video files, as doing so would disclose proprietary information and violate our employee privacy policies.”

I bet that wasn't the first time that CRC specialist fired off that exact quote. Really weird thing for an innocent giant card grading company to say and do (or be unable to do). Their refusal to provide this evidence is extremely telling. It is in fact physically impossible for them to provide this evidence, because I submitted a pristine English card. The Japanese card is not one I have ever owned, nor, frankly, one my brother would have ever bought. He has been using PSA to grade cards for over a decade — or rather, he was. Unlike me, he takes pictures of his cards because he loves them more than I do, but he never photographed his cards before submitting them to PSA or any other grading company, never suspecting the kind of foul play I have experienced from PSA. We were both deeply shocked and dismayed by this behavior from PSA, and we will never proceed with such trust again. Thank you, PSA, for the expensive lesson!

This site will be updated with more details as I continue to pursue justice for the swapping (i.e., theft) of my $1,000 card by PSA.

PSA at first told me, basically, “Tough crap, we're grading this Japanese card — it's what you sent — and we're charging you for it.” Then, after the formal dispute with PSA's parent company, that changed to, “We'll charge you to ship you back your card.” That's not my card. Thanks. I don't know who at PSA took my card, but it's long gone from my possession, though I owned it for so long. Good times. Rather than pay them for the shitty consolation prize of the card they swapped mine with, I have invested in posting my truth, which, unless I'm hallucinating (beyond what neuroscientist Anil Seth considers the norm), is in fact truth/reality. PSA sucks. That has been 100% of my experience with them.

I can't believe I didn't take photographs.” The PSA rep on the phone literally laughed at me and said, more or less, “Yep, you need to do that.” In other words, even PSA admitted over the phone that they are not trustworthy enough to send things to without thoroughly documenting what you're sending in to protect yourself.
