PSA Swaps Cards

How one customer lost a ~$1,000 card on his first-ever PSA submission, and why you should photograph everything before you send it.

What happened

I submitted one card to PSA for grading: an English-language Kingdom Hearts TCG Cloud Holo Foil Super Rare (31/91SR), in pristine condition and worth about $1,000. My brother gave it to me about 20 years ago, and it was the only Kingdom Hearts card I've ever owned.

PSA says the card in my package was a Japanese Cloud in significantly worse condition, worth under $100. I have never owned that card.

The timeline

  • August 2026: I submitted my English Cloud card.
  • On arrival: PSA's site listed my card as Japanese. I filed an error report immediately.
  • About a month later: PSA hadn't responded, and its site showed my card moving into grading. I called. The rep told me, “Yeah, your card's Japanese.” I requested an investigation.
  • The investigation: PSA concluded that I had sent a Japanese card, and said it would grade that card and charge me for it.
  • Formal dispute: I filed a formal dispute with PSA's parent company. PSA sent blurry still photos with no timestamps, showing a Japanese card and my USPS box. It refused to provide the one thing that would actually prove its claim: video of my sealed box being opened and the card coming out.
  • Final position: PSA offered to ship me the Japanese card if I paid for shipping. I declined. It isn't my card.

The evidence PSA won't show

PSA says its intake footage shows a Japanese card coming out of my box. When I asked to see that footage, PSA replied:

“We are unable to share unedited video files, as doing so would disclose proprietary information and violate our employee privacy policies.”

If the footage showed what PSA says it shows, PSA could end this dispute by showing it. It hasn't, and that tells you something. PSA cannot produce video of my box yielding a Japanese card, because I never sent one.

What PSA told me on the phone

When PSA told me the investigation was closed and I had “sent a Japanese card,” I said, “You can't be serious. I can't believe I didn't take photographs.”

The rep laughed and said, more or less, “Yep, you need to do that.”

So even PSA's own staff will tell you not to trust PSA with a card you haven't documented.

My conclusion

My English card went into PSA's custody and did not come back. Based on everything above, I conclude that my card was swapped while in PSA's possession. In plain terms, I conclude that my card was stolen. I'm pursuing this through every legal means available, and this page will be updated as it develops.

TAKE PHOTOS. Photograph and video every card, front and back, before you ship it, and record yourself packing the box. If trusting that your card will be handled properly matters to you, I'd strongly recommend not using PSA.

Ask yourself

Do I have some strange incentive to invent this story and pay for websites to tell it? Or does PSA, whose entire business depends on trust, have a massive incentive to conceal a swap and withhold the evidence?

Tough call. 😑
